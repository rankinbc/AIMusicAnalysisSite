## Explain
The counterintuitive rule: a *fast* compressor attack can kill punch, a
*slower* attack can add it. The first ~10-30 ms of a drum hit is the "snap"
your ear reads as punch. A fast attack clamps that spike immediately and the
drum sounds smaller; a slower attack (≈10-30 ms) lets the transient through and
the compressor works on the body behind it — preserving, even increasing, the
contrast between the peak and the sustain. Use a fast attack only when a
transient is genuinely too loud and you want to tame it.

## Anchor to your track
A healthy crest factor and solid transient strength mean your hits still punch —
protect that. If both are low, transients are already being clamped; slowing
attacks (or backing off compression) is the way back.

## Genre notes
Faster transients (drums) tolerate faster attacks than slow sources (pads,
vocals). For four-on-the-floor genres, set the release so the gain reduction
recovers just before the next kick so the groove breathes.

## When it's the wrong tool
A high ratio with a very fast attack is the *least* punchy setting of all — it
just sounds dull. If you want weight without losing snap, reach for parallel
compression instead of clamping harder.
