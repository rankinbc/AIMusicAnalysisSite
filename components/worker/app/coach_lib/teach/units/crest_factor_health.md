## Explain
Two different rulers tell you about dynamics. Crest factor (peak minus average,
in dB) measures transient health: unprocessed drums sit around 16-18 dB, a
well-balanced master often 8-12 dB, and a crushed master collapses toward 3-6
dB. Loudness range measures the macro spread between quiet and loud sections; a
range near zero means your sections stopped contrasting. Crest is about the
snap; loudness range is about the build-to-drop arc — watch both.

## Anchor to your track
Read your measured crest factor and loudness range together. Crest down near
3-6 dB means transients are being crushed; a near-flat loudness range means
section contrast is gone. If you didn't intend either, back off the squashing.

## Genre notes
Dancefloor genres run a tighter loudness range by design, and that's fine —
just don't drive it to zero or the drop stops landing. Trance/progressive need
the most room to protect breakdown-to-drop dynamics.

## When it's the wrong tool
These are diagnostic numbers, not targets to dial. They tell you *whether*
you've over-processed; the sound is still the arbiter. Don't compress to hit a
crest number — use the number to catch yourself going too far.
