## Explain
"Glue" compression on the mix bus makes separate elements feel like one record.
The tradition aims for just 1-3 dB of gain reduction (4 dB max) with a low ratio
(around 2:1), a slow-ish attack (10-30 ms) so transients pass, and an auto or
medium release that breathes with the program. A sidechain high-pass around
80-150 Hz stops the kick from over-triggering it. Past ~4 dB you start bleeding
the punch out of kick and snare — and that damage can't be undone later.

## Anchor to your track
Glue should barely move your crest factor. If your crest is already low, the bus
is doing too much — pull the glue back rather than adding more.

## Genre notes
Trance/progressive want light glue (1.5:1-2:1, 2-3 dB) so the build-to-drop
contrast survives. Techno tolerates a slightly firmer hand, but the same "less
is more" ceiling applies.

## When it's the wrong tool
Don't mix into a master-bus limiter or heavy glue — it skews your perception and
makes you chase your tail. Build the balance first, add gentle glue last.
