## Explain
Harshness — the abrasive, fatiguing edge — lives around 2-5 kHz, which is also
where your ear is most sensitive. Treat it with a gentle, moderate-width cut of
1-3 dB at the offending frequency. If the harshness only spikes on louder
moments, a dynamic cut (one that engages only when that band crosses a
threshold) keeps the brightness during softer passages and only tames the
peaks. Harshness around 5-8 kHz from vocals or hats is a separate problem — a
de-esser, not a broad cut.

## Anchor to your track
A high upper-mid band reading and a high spectral centroid together point to a
top-heavy, edgy balance — use them to confirm the harshness is real and not
just monitoring.

## Genre notes
Trance supersaw leads commonly get edgy in the 3-4 kHz range; a small gentle cut
there tames them. Techno harshness usually comes from hats, ride loops and
saturated stabs — treat the percussion bus rather than the whole mix.

## When it's the wrong tool
Don't fix harshness by adding saturation — saturation adds harmonics and makes
it worse. If bypassing your plugin chain removes the edge, the cause is your
processing, not a missing cut.
