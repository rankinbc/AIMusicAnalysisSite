## Explain
Almost everything that isn't the kick or bass has low-frequency energy it
doesn't need — pads, leads, FX, vocals. High-passing those channels clears
headroom and unclogs the low end so the real bass elements breathe. Rough
starting corners: vocals 80-120 Hz, pads 100-200 Hz, hats 200-400 Hz, all at a
gentle 12 dB/oct so it sounds natural. Sweep each cutoff up while the full mix
plays until the part thins, then back off.

## Anchor to your track
High overall low-frequency energy together with a hot low-mid band often means
clutter from many non-bass parts rather than a single bass problem — cleaning
the periphery is usually the bigger win than touching the bass itself.

## Genre notes
Trance high-passes non-bass parts fairly aggressively (80-120 Hz). Techno
sometimes high-passes a little less to preserve a raw, full character — but
still keeps everything below ~120-150 Hz mono.

## When it's the wrong tool
Over-high-passing thins sounds and removes the body that glues a mix. A part
that sounds thin soloed can sit perfectly in the mix — judge in context, not in
solo.
