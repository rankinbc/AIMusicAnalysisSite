## Explain
Kick and bass both live in the low end and mask each other when they share the
same frequencies at the same level. The classic static fix is complementary
EQ: pick which element owns the sub, boost it there, and cut the other at the
same spot. If the kick's fundamental is ~60 Hz, make a small 1.5-4 dB dip in
the bass at 60 Hz (Q 2-4), and dip the kick a touch where the bass fundamental
sits. You carve a window for each instead of letting them pile up.

## Anchor to your track
A hot bass band combined with high overall low-frequency energy is the
signature of two low-end sources competing — that's your cue to carve
complementary slots rather than turning either one down wholesale.

## Genre notes
With a bass root of A (~55 Hz), dip the kick around 55 Hz and 110 Hz to open a
window for the sub. Techno usually decides early whether the kick or the
bassline carries the sub and carves the other out hard.

## When it's the wrong tool
At the mixed/rendered stage you can't separate the two perfectly with EQ. If
they overlap rhythmically, a sidechain duck (bass ducking under the kick) does
what EQ can't. If they play the same note at the same time, it's arrangement.
