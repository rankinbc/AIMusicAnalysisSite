## Explain
A brickwall limiter should only catch the last 1-3 dB. If you need 6-10 dB of
reduction just to feel competitive, or the mix crumbles and pumps when you push
it, the limiter is overdriven and the fix is upstream. Lower the crest factor
*before* the limiter: control uncontrolled low end (the number-one culprit),
add clipping or saturation to raise the average-to-peak ratio, use multiband
control so one loud kick doesn't duck the whole spectrum, and stage your
loudness so the brickwall does almost nothing.

## Anchor to your track
Read loudness, crest factor and true peak together. A loud integrated loudness
with a very low crest factor and a pinned true peak is the signature of a
limiter doing too much heavy lifting — relieve it upstream rather than pushing
harder.

## Genre notes
Big-room and festival material leans hardest on clipping and saturation
*before* the limiter so the brickwall stays gentle. Trance/progressive should
keep the limiter especially light to protect the breakdown-to-drop swing.

## When it's the wrong tool
More limiter gain is never the answer to a mix that won't get loud cleanly. The
signs you've gone too far — pumping, a dull/brittle top, flattened transients —
all say "fix the crest factor upstream," not "push the limiter."
