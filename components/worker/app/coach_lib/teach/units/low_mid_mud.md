## Explain
Mud is a traffic jam in the low-mids, roughly 200-500 Hz, where pads, bass
harmonics, kick body and rhythm synths all pile up and smear the mix. The fix
is small, spread-out subtractive cuts — 2-4 dB with a moderate-width bell
(Q around 1) on the offending tracks — not one big scoop on the master. Find
the worst spot by boosting a narrow bell and sweeping until it sounds boxy,
then cut there. Many small cuts across a few tracks beat one large cut.

## Anchor to your track
Compare your measured low-mid band against your mid band. If the low-mid energy
sits clearly hotter than the mid, that's the buildup you can hear — point the
cut at the band that's running hot.

## Genre notes
Techno carves 250-500 Hz harder because the arrangement is sparse and the low
end has to stay clean and driving. Trance keeps a deliberate mid-bass character
layer around 100-400 Hz, so go gentler there or you thin out the body.

## When it's the wrong tool
If the mix only muds up when several parts play at once, it's an arrangement or
sidechain problem, not EQ — thin the arrangement or duck the clashers instead
of cutting every track.
