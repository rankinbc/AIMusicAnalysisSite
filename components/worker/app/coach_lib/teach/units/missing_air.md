## Explain
"Air" is the open, sparkly top above ~10 kHz. Add it with a gentle high shelf,
corner around 10-12 kHz, +1.5 to +4 dB — a shelf sounds more natural than a
narrow boost here. The catch: EQ can only lift what already exists. If the
source genuinely has no high-frequency content, a shelf just amplifies hiss and
turns harsh. In that case generate new highs with saturation or a harmonic
exciter instead of boosting.

## Anchor to your track
A low air-band reading together with a low spectral centroid means the balance
really is dark — a shelf will help. If the air band is already healthy, the
dullness is elsewhere and a top boost will just add noise.

## Genre notes
Trance breakdowns and supersaw leads can take generous air (+2 to +4 dB) to feel
big and emotional. Techno stays more restrained — a little on the hat/ride bus
and master is enough; over-bright techno fatigues on big club systems.

## When it's the wrong tool
If a top boost only raises hiss, the high content is absent — switch to
saturation/excitation. And don't chase air on a mix that's actually muddy:
clear the low-mids first, then decide if you still need the top.
