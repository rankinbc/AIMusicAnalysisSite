## Explain
Over-compression flattens the contrast that gives music life: dull transients,
verse and chorus hitting at the same intensity, audible pumping, a fatiguing
"squashed" sound. Diagnose it with numbers, not vibes — crest factor (peak
minus average) and loudness range (section-to-section spread). When a mix
sounds wrong the fix is almost always *less*: raise the threshold, lower the
ratio, remove a compressor, fix the release timing, or switch heavy serial
compression for parallel compression. Sometimes the right move is bypassing the
compressor entirely.

## Anchor to your track
A low crest factor and a collapsed loudness range together are the fingerprint
of an over-squashed master. Watch crest fall in real time as you add
compression — that's a direct warning you're flattening transients.

## Genre notes
Trance and progressive live on build-to-drop contrast, so protect the loudness
range — over-compressing the bus kills the drop's impact. Techno tolerates
tighter, more constant compression, but density should be earned in the mix,
not stamped on by a limiter.

## When it's the wrong tool
Even 3 dB of gain reduction can pump if attack/release are wrong — the problem
isn't always the *amount*. Gain-match and bypass before deciding: if the quieter
"before" actually sounds more alive, use less.
