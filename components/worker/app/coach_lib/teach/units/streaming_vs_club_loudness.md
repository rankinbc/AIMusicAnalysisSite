## Explain
Streaming services turn loud masters *down* to a reference level (around -14
LUFS on Spotify, YouTube, Tidal and Amazon; -16 on Apple). So pushing past that
target buys you nothing on streaming — a -6 LUFS master and a -14 LUFS master
play back at the same loudness, except the loud one already threw away its
dynamics. The honest move is to earn density in the arrangement and mix, hit a
reasonable integrated loudness, and let the platform normalise. Club/DJ masters
are a separate, hotter case (roughly -8 to -6 LUFS) because that playback isn't
normalised.

## Anchor to your track
Compare your measured integrated loudness to the streaming reference. If you're
already at or past it, going louder only costs you dynamic range — the platform
will undo the gain anyway.

## Genre notes
EDM is mastered hot, and that's fine — a loud master turned down still keeps the
saturation and transient energy baked in. The point isn't "be quiet", it's
"don't sacrifice dynamics chasing a number the platform ignores."

## When it's the wrong tool
Don't treat -14 LUFS as a hard mastering target — it's a normalization
reference, not a rule. If your track is sparse and sub-driven, trust your ears
and a reference track over the LUFS number, which under-weights sub bass.
