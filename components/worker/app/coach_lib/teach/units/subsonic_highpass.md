## Explain
Energy below ~20-30 Hz is mostly felt, not heard, and it quietly steals
headroom and pushes your limiter into odd behaviour. A steep high-pass near
20-30 Hz on the master clears subsonic rumble and DC offset and gives you a
hotter, cleaner level for free. On individual non-bass tracks, high-pass higher
(see the dedicated lesson). On bass-bearing elements use a steeper slope
(24-48 dB/oct) so you protect headroom without thinning the usable low end.

## Anchor to your track
A high overall low-frequency energy reading with a thin-sounding result is a
classic sign of wasted sub energy below the musical range — a subsonic cut
recovers headroom you can put toward audible weight.

## Genre notes
Trance has little useful content below ~35 Hz, so cut there confidently. Techno
often runs a lower, sustained sub, so high-pass more conservatively (around
30 Hz on the bass) to keep maximum weight.

## When it's the wrong tool
A steep high-pass adds phase shift and can soften transients near the corner —
don't crank the slope on transient-critical elements. Sweep the cutoff up while
the full mix plays until it thins, then back off.
