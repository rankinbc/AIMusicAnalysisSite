## Explain
Weight and body live around 60-120 Hz; "boom" is too much of the same region,
and 200-250 Hz adds a boxy "honk". For a thin low end, add a wide, gentle boost
of 2-3 dB around 70-100 Hz — meters jump fast down here, so re-check your level
after. For a boomy low end, cut 2-4 dB around 60-120 Hz, and/or tilt the very
bottom down with a low shelf. Decide first which element owns the sub (usually
kick or bass) and shape the other around it.

## Anchor to your track
Read your bass-band energy alongside the overall low-frequency energy. Low on
both means thin (boost); high on both means boomy (cut). Let the numbers tell
you which direction before you reach for a move.

## Genre notes
Trance runs a three-part low end — kick transient, a clean sine sub, and a
mid-bass character layer — so boom usually comes from kick/sub overlap around
55-110 Hz. Techno often rides a long sustained sub, and boom comes from
too-long kick tails overlapping the bass.

## When it's the wrong tool
If boosting the low end eats all your headroom and makes the limiter pump, the
problem is gain-staging or an uncontrolled sub, not a missing boost — tame the
low end first, then add weight.
